## Overview

New to robots? Start here. Robotics 101 is a short, friendly introduction to what robots are, where they came from and what goes into building one. There's no code and no hardware needed, just curiosity.

## Course Content

In this course you will learn:

* What is a Robot - a definition
* A quick history of robotics
* The skills and knowledge you need to build robots
* The different parts of a robot
* How a robot "thinks" - the sense, think, act loop

## Key Results

By the end of this course you'll be able to:

* Explain what makes a machine a robot
* Name some famous robots from history
* Describe the main parts of a robot: brain, body, power, sensors, motors and motor driver
* Describe how a robot "thinks" using the sense, think, act loop
* Pick your next course from the Build Robots learning pathway

## What you'll need

* Nothing but a web browser! Each lesson ends with a short quiz.
* Optional: pen and paper for the Try it Yourself exercises

## How the course works

Each lesson is short and has lots of pictures. Work through them in order, and try the quiz at the end of each one to check your understanding.

Every lesson has a **Try it Yourself** section with a few things to do away from the screen, and a **Check your understanding** quiz. Your quiz score shows in the lesson sidebar, and it is stored in your own browser only.

## Where to go next

Robotics 101 is the first step on the Build Robots learning pathway. A good order is:

- [Electronics basics](/learn/electronics_basics/) - voltage, current, breadboards and your first circuits
- [Build a SMARS Robot](/learn/smars/) - a 3D-printed tracked robot you can build yourself
- [Motors and motor drivers](/learn/motors_and_drivers/) - how robots really move
- [Learn MicroPython - The basics](/learn/micropython/) - programming for the Raspberry Pi Pico

> **Teachers**: This course suits ages 10 and up, and complete beginners of any age. Each lesson takes about 15 to 20 minutes, so the whole course fits in one or two sessions. It needs no hardware, accounts or software installs, so it works on locked-down school computers and tablets; the video may need YouTube unblocking, but every lesson works without it. For a group activity, bring a real robot, toy or kit to hold up while you read the lessons, Batteries, soldering irons and tools only appear in the follow-on courses, where each has its own safety notes.
